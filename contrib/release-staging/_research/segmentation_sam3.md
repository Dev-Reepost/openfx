# segmentation_sam3 — Upstream Research Brief

## Summary

SAM 3 (Segment Anything Model 3) is Meta AI Research's unified segmentation model that detects, segments, and tracks objects in images and video given a **text prompt** (a short noun phrase like "yellow school bus"), an **image exemplar**, or a **click/box prompt**. The OFX plugin sends a clip plus prompt to a ComfyUI server, which runs SAM 3 to produce a per-frame alpha mask propagated across the sequence. Typical VFX use case: rapid rotoscoping for sky replacement, character isolation, garbage-matte generation, or driving a secondary grade — work that previously required frame-by-frame manual roto.

## Technical approach

SAM 3 introduces **Promptable Concept Segmentation (PCS)**: an open-vocabulary text or exemplar prompt yields *all* matching instances rather than a single object. Architecturally it uses a **decoupled detector–tracker** sharing a single vision encoder; SAM 3.1 adds a shared-memory mechanism for joint multi-object tracking. Compared with **SAM 1** (2023, click/box prompts on still images) and **SAM 2** (2024, video memory bank for click-prompted propagation), SAM 3 is the first in the family to accept **natural-language prompts** and to detect+track simultaneously. For VFX rotoscoping, mask propagation through a clip from a single prompt is the killer feature — one text phrase or click drives a temporally consistent matte across hundreds of frames.

## Requirements & limitations

- **VRAM:** Inference fits in ~4 GB; image segmentation runs comfortably on 16 GB consumer GPUs (RTX 4090). 4K end-to-end requires 24 GB+. Real-time multi-object video at high FPS targets H100/H200-class GPUs.
- **Throughput:** ~30 ms per image (H200, 100+ objects); SAM 3.1 reaches 32 FPS on H100 for medium-object-count video. Latency scales with object count; real-time sustained for ~5 concurrent objects.
- **Clip length / propagation pass:** <UNVERIFIED>No hard upper bound documented; quality degrades on long clips (boundary drift observable past several hundred frames).</UNVERIFIED>
- **Resolution:** No fixed cap; higher resolutions increase VRAM linearly.
- **Known failure modes:** long-duration occlusion (loses track and fails to recover after objects reappear), identity swaps between visually similar instances, boundary drift under slow lighting changes, hallucinated false positives under fast motion/motion-blur, weakness on very small/thin objects, dense overlapping instances. Reported video accuracy is meaningfully below image accuracy (cgF1 30.3% vs 54.1% on SA-Co).
